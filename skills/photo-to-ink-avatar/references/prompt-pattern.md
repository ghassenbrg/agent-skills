# Human cartoon avatar brief

Fill in source-specific cues. The approved image supplies style, not the person's identity.

```text
Use case: style-transfer. Turn image 1 into an expressive human ink cartoon avatar.
Image 1: source person's identity, face shape, hair, accessories, outfit,
head angle, and expression. Image 2: style and avatar composition only.
Canvas: [square head-and-shoulders portrait or requested format].
Preserve: [distinctive facial proportions], [hairline and hairstyle],
[brow shape/facial hair/glasses/accessories], [source outfit], [head angle].
Do not copy the person, haircut, or jacket from image 2.

Draw a recognizable simplified human face with a defined jaw and ears,
bold brows, rounded black eyes with small cream glints, confident eyelid
strokes, a simple curved-stroke nose, and [source-appropriate expression].
Keep the face cream-white and largely flat. Use bold black contours and
large expressive hair shapes with a few cream highlight gaps. Render the
neck, shoulders, and source clothing as natural human cartoon shapes,
using black ink masses, cream negative space, and minimal fold/collar lines.
No circular blank mascot face, stick body, anime eyes, or realistic skin shading.

Warm off-white paper, a broad irregular golden-yellow dry-brush halo,
and vivid orange outline doodles [chosen motifs] around the outer margins.
Match image 2's confident ink line weight, restrained brush texture,
and ragged painted edges at the shoulder base. Keep icons away from the face.
The face is the clear focal point, with profile-circle-safe hair and chin.
No photo UI, added slogans, logos, watermark, glossy 3D, photorealism,
soft airbrushed painting, rainbow palette, or generic slick vector rendering.
```

When retaining a photo's scene, replace the halo/background paragraph with a brief naming the actual setting and requesting simplified, low-contrast ink shapes. For a requested theme, replace the doodle motifs with a few relevant simple orange icons.
