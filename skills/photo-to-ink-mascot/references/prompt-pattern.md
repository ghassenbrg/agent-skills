# Photo transformation brief

Replace bracketed fields using the actual photo. The approved example is a style reference, never a template for the person's identity or setting.

```text
Use case: style-transfer. Transform image 1 into an abstract ink mascot illustration.
Image 1: source person, hairstyle, outfit, accessories, pose, framing, and setting.
Image 2: approved abstraction, character shapes, brush texture, and palette only.
Canvas: [source aspect ratio/crop or requested format].
Preserve these recognition cues: [hair silhouette and parting], [clothing category],
[visible accessories], [gesture/head angle], [key setting elements].

Simplify the ENTIRE person into the rounded mascot grammar of image 2:
oversized cream-white circular face with a thick organic charcoal outline,
minimal solid oval/stroke eyes, [appropriate expression], simple black mouth
with orange interior if open. No realistic nose, lips, lashes, irises, facial
anatomy, skin shading, or detailed body contours. Use a compact simple torso,
curved brush limbs, rounded hands, flat outfit shapes, and very few fold marks.
Hair is a few broad expressive brush silhouettes, not individual strands.
Keep this person's identifying cues; do not copy image 2's hair, outfit or scene.

Warm cream paper, charcoal-black brush ink, selective vivid orange accents,
tiny golden highlights and restrained gray. Visible dry-brush texture,
confident rounded shapes, mostly flat 2D. Translate [source setting] into
quiet background brush marks and swashes with generous cream negative space.
Preserve the requested people and composition. No extra characters or props.
Remove capture UI and incidental signage text unless requested. No new text,
logos, watermark, anime, realistic painted portrait, glossy 3D, or slick vectors.
```

For realism drift, append: "A realistically drawn person with a cartoon face pasted on is incorrect. Simplify the head, hair, torso, limbs, hands, and clothing into the same abstract brush-shape language."
